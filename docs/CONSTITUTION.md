# Project Harness Constitution

1. **Customer intent remains attributable.** Preserve raw requests and supplied artifacts; record interpretation and derived requirements separately.
2. **Waterfall stages provide detail; agile revision keeps them current.** Discovery, requirements, design, planning, implementation, verification, acceptance, deployment, closure, and maintenance have explicit inputs, deliverables, dependencies, and exit criteria. Inputs may arrive in any order and a completed stage may be revisited. Iterations establish the necessary upstream basis for their scope before dependent implementation; every implementation change has a TASK in a currently approved PLAN. Apply the stage and change rules in [Development method](DEVELOPMENT_METHOD.md).
3. **Uncertainty stays visible and fails closed.** Unknowns, conflicts, assumptions, and ambiguous evidence remain active open items until an authorized resolution is propagated and evidenced.
4. **Authority is explicit and proportionate.** A current human PLAN approval authorizes routine execution of the planned TASK; a user request authorizes discovery and planning but is never a substitute for that decision. Cryptographic human approval is reserved for production, production data, destructive operations, secret access, and final customer acceptance; agents never self-grant any human decision.
5. **Progress is evidence-based.** Code existence is not verification, verification is not customer acceptance, and deployment is not closure.
6. **Baselines are versioned and revisable.** Requirements, plans, and deliverables may change throughout the project. A change to agreed requirements, acceptance criteria, scope or material constraints needs a change record, impact analysis, preserved prior version, and an identifiable replacement version. Task refinement and implementation corrections within the agreement use their task trace and evidence without automatically requiring a separate business decision. Renew authority wherever the current runtime invalidates it and renew affected verification before relying on changed outputs.
7. **Collaboration preserves ownership.** A single writer works directly. Concurrent writers use exact handoffs or isolated worktrees with non-overlapping path and logical-resource claims.
8. **Stop work according to impact and actual authority.** Missing inputs, changed baselines, or unresolved decisions block their dependents; unrelated prepared work may continue only while its own gates and approval remain current. With the current single-level PLAN model, changing the approved PLAN or its bound scope invalidates authority for every TASK in that PLAN. Impact analysis does not override this boundary.
9. **Recorded attempts and failures are retained.** When an audited result workflow is used, the latest attempt controls it. Failed, incomplete, stale, superseded, and rejected records remain history.
10. **Names are not identities.** Agent and reviewer IDs provide procedural provenance; hostile separation and protected execution belong to an external orchestrator or CI boundary.
11. **Derived views are disposable.** Human reports and `HANDOFF.md` may be regenerated; canonical records, shared runtime state, Git history, and protected authority records keep their distinct roles.
12. **Responsible uncertainty beats fabricated certainty.** When a completion claim cannot be proven at the required verification profile, the correct state is unresolved.
13. **Two inputs carry a project.** What the customer supplied and what the developer says to the agent are the only inputs a project needs from intake to closure; the agent derives every record, gate, and report from them. The owner's decision is one of those prompts. The only step outside them is the signature on a high-risk request, and that is by design.
14. **Readiness reviews do not multiply approvals.** The agent checks stage exit criteria and presents progress, evidence, and unresolved items. Use the existing consolidated PLAN decision and high-risk approvals; request a separate stage sign-off only when the project's explicit contract requires it. A stage label, iteration review, or demonstration does not itself grant execution authority or customer acceptance.
15. **Plans organize work and remain adjustable.** Requirements give rise to tasks whose detail and order may be determined during planning or adjusted to user priorities. The agent may add, split and reorder work within the agreed outcomes and constraints, respecting technical prerequisites and explaining material tradeoffs. Routine refinement is distinct from changing the agreement. The current runtime's broader fingerprint binding remains a documented constraint until implementation changes it.
